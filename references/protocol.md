## Resume & Cover Letter Master Framework

---

> Before generating any document, read this file in full.

## PHASE I: INITIALIZATION — STRATEGIC ANALYSIS

### Step 1: Job Description Deconstruction

Before touching the resume, read the job description as a document about organizational
need — not a checklist of requirements.

**The orienting question:** *What problem does this organization need solved, and why
is this the hire that solves it?*

**Tiered Keyword Classification**

| Tier | Inclusion | What belongs here |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| Tier 1 | 100% | Job title, required tools (Jira, MS Project, SAFe), methodologies (Agile, Waterfall, OKR), hard certs (PMP), industry terms |
| Tier 2 | 80% | Preferred qualifications, soft skills (stakeholder management), domain experience |
| Tier 3 | 50% | Company values, nice-to-have skills, adjacent competencies |

**Additional Analysis**

- **Problem Statement:** What specific organizational problem does this role address?
  Name it in one sentence. Every positioning decision flows from this.
- **Seniority Indicators:** Note budget scale, team size, reporting structure.
  These calibrate the stakes language in bullets.
- **Gap Analysis (Adjacency Rule):** Map where the person's experience doesn't
  directly match — then identify adjacent successes that demonstrate the same
  capability in a different domain.

**Output before proceeding:**
- KEY REQUIREMENTS (ranked)
- TIERED KEYWORD LIST (with JD frequency)
- PROBLEM STATEMENT (one sentence)
- GAP ANALYSIS (strong alignment vs. creative positioning)

*If the person seems overwhelmed by the gap analysis results — pause here.
Check in before continuing. See Support Mode in SKILL.md.*

---

### Step 2: Experience Mapping

Start from what you already know. If the person has shared context in this conversation
or previously, use it — don't ask for what you already have. Begin with what's established
and ask only for what's missing.

**Metric Hardening**

For each role, collect:
- Before vs. After data (what changed because of this work)
- Scale markers: budget owned, team size, user count, geographic scope
- Timeline: how long did it take, what was the pressure
- What would have failed without this person specifically

**Sequential Questioning Protocol**

Ask about one requirement at a time. Wait for the response before moving to the next.
Do not front-load a list of questions. The intake is a conversation, not a form.

*If the person has trouble quantifying their impact — this is common and normal.
Prompt with: "What changed after you did this? Even roughly — bigger, faster, fewer
problems, more people served?" The number doesn't have to be exact. It has to be honest.*

## PHASE II: CONTENT GENERATION

### Step 3: The Victory Bullet Formula

**Formula:**
`[Strong Action Verb] + [Problem/Risk Inherited] + [Solution/Approach] + [Trade-off Navigated] + [Quantified Impact]`

**ATS Constraints**
- Max 160 characters per bullet (including spaces)
- If an achievement exceeds 160 characters: split into two bullets.
  Lead with the outcome. Follow with the method.
- Run the character count. State it. Never guess.

**Action Verb Bank**

| Register | Verbs |
|----------|-------|
| Strategic | Architected, Navigated, Orchestrated, Restructured, Transformed |
| Leadership | Championed, Spearheaded, Mobilized, Galvanized, Drove |
| Governance | Instituted, Formalized, Aligned, Enforced, Owned |

**Avoid:** Facilitated, Led, Managed, Helped, Supported — these describe participation,
not ownership. The bullet should name what would have failed without this person.

---

### Step 3A: Ownership Validation (Mandatory)

Every bullet must pass the Three-Question Test before it goes in the document:

1. **SO WHAT** — What was the consequence of this action?
2. **COMPARED TO WHAT** — What's the baseline this beat?
3. **WHO CARES** — Which stakeholder does this matter to?

**Trade-off Variety**

Across a role's bullets, show different tensions:
- Speed vs. Quality
- Autonomy vs. Standardization
- Delivery pressure vs. Stakeholder alignment
- Short-term fix vs. Long-term infrastructure

Bullets that all show the same kind of win read as one-dimensional. Variety signals
someone who navigates complexity, not just executes tasks.

## PHASE III: DOCUMENT ARCHITECTURE

### Step 4: Summary & Core Competencies

**Professional Summary**
- 3–4 lines
- Must pass the 6-second scan: job title and years of experience visible immediately
- Include top 5 Tier 1 keywords
- No clichés. No apologies for the arc. No "results-driven professional."

**Core Competencies**
- 2–3 columns, 12–18 items
- Include ALL Tier 1 and top 10 Tier 2 keywords
- Top-left position = highest priority JD keyword

---

### Step 5: Cover Letter — T-Shape Structure

**Constraint:** 450 words maximum. No exceptions.

**Structure (5 moves, in order):**

1. **Specific Fit Opener** — Name the role and one precise reason this person is
   the right fit. Not enthusiasm. Evidence.
2. **Primary Qualification + Quantified Result** — The strongest alignment with
   the core problem this role addresses. One example, fully landed.
3. **Cross-Domain Differentiator** — What the non-linear career brings that a
   conventional candidate can't. Frame it as an asset, not an explanation.
4. **Contextual Investment** — Why this organization, this moment. One specific
   detail that couldn't appear in a letter to anyone else.
5. **Clear Close** — Availability, next step, one line. No groveling.

**Prohibited in cover letters:**
- Apologies for gaps, pivots, or a non-linear path
- Clichés (see Banned Phrases below)
- Summaries of the resume — the cover letter adds a layer, not a recap
- Vague enthusiasm ("I am excited to bring my passion for...")

## PHASE IV: FORMATTING & ATS COMPLIANCE

### Step 6: Employment History & Dates

**Date Format:** Month YYYY – Month YYYY (e.g., January 2018 – Present). Always.

**Gap Prevention:**
- No unexplained gaps > 2 months
- Acceptable gap framing: "Strategic career transition" or "Consulting / Contract work"
- Ensure dates are logically consecutive — ATS systems flag gaps automatically

**Non-Linear Career Framing:**
The pivot is not the problem. The framing is. Each role gets one thread pulled through
it — the thread most relevant to the target role. Adjacent experience is an asset when
named correctly. It is a liability only when left unnamed or apologized for.

---

### Step 7: Technical Standardization

**File:** Standard headers. No tables, text boxes, or columns except in the
Competencies section. Final format is determined at delivery — see Step 9.

**The Fold Rule:** Top one-third of page one must contain:
- Name
- Job title that matches the target role
- Core value proposition
- At least 2 major metrics

**Language Standards:**
- Passive voice < 5%
- Spell out acronyms on first use, then abbreviate
- Both the acronym and the spelled-out term must appear at least once

**Document Standards:**
- Length: 1.75 pages maximum
- Bullets: ~17–20 total across all roles
- Character limit: ~7,500 total
- Font: Arial or equivalent clean sans-serif
- Minimum 3 metrics per role

## PHASE V: QUALITY ASSURANCE & DELIVERY

### Step 8: QA Checklist

Run every item before delivery. Report any failures before sending.

**Technical**
- [ ] 100% of bullets ≤ 160 characters (run the count — do not estimate)
- [ ] All dates include Month YYYY; no gaps > 2 months
- [ ] Zero tables, text boxes, or columns outside Competencies section
- [ ] .docx format confirmed
- [ ] Top fold contains name, title, value prop, 2+ metrics

**Content**
- [ ] 100% Tier 1 keywords present
- [ ] Minimum 3 metrics per role
- [ ] Every bullet passes the Three-Question Test
- [ ] Cover letter ≤ 450 words
- [ ] Cover letter follows T-shape structure (5 moves)
- [ ] No prohibited phrases (see below)
- [ ] No apologies for career arc anywhere in document

**Anti-AI Writing Check**

Apply to cover letter before delivery. Resume bullets follow their own formula,
but cover letters carry voice — and AI-generated voice is identifiable and
disqualifying at the hiring manager level.

Banned phrases — zero tolerance:

`at its core` · `here's the kicker` · `this underscores` · `key takeaway` ·
`looking ahead` · `at the end of the day` · `to put it simply` · `in other words` ·
`broadly speaking` · `generally speaking` · `for context` · `digging deeper` ·
`in today's digital age` · `on one hand... on the other` · `it's worth noting` ·
`that being said` · `from a broader perspective` · `in conclusion`

Additional prohibited: `ground zero` · intensifier padding (`truly`, `genuinely`,
`incredibly`, `deeply` used as filler) · passive constructions that hide ownership

**The register test:** Does this cover letter sound like a person wrote it,
or like a person asked a machine to write it? If the answer is uncertain,
rewrite the opener and the close. Those are the two places AI voice concentrates.

---

### Step 9: Delivery

**Format**

Before generating any document, ask:

> "Before I put this together — how would you like to receive the documents?
>
> - **Word (.docx)** — recommended for most people; opens in Microsoft Word or Google Docs
> - **Plain text (.txt)** — works on any device, easy to copy and paste
> - **Markdown (.md)** — best if you use a notes app or writing tool that supports it"

If the person is unsure, recommend Word.

**Standard delivery package:**

1. **Resume** — QA-passed, ATS-optimized, in chosen format
2. **Cover Letter** — T-shape, ≤ 450 words, anti-AI check passed, in chosen format---
